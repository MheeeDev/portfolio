## Problem

A large public hospital runs lighting, air-conditioning and medical equipment around the clock, but
its facilities team saw only one figure each month: the utility bill for the whole site. Nobody could
say which building used what, when the load peaked, or what moving work to off-peak hours would save.

## My role & scope

I was the project engineer for the energy-monitoring part:

- **Metering** — CTs on L1, L2 and L3 of each monitored board into Siemens SENTRON PAC5200 meters,
  with CT ratio, phase and polarity checked against a clamp meter
- **Data** — meters on the building Ethernet, read by Siemens Energy Manager PRO
- **Money** — TOU and TOD cost formulas set up to match the utility tariff
- **Screens** — the hospital dashboard designed in Energy Manager PRO's web client

## Approach

- **Measured, not estimated.** Each PAC5200 combines bus voltage with CT current and works out kW,
  kVAR, power factor and kWh itself. Energy Manager PRO stores those readings as time series.
- **Priced like the utility.** Large-service tariff 4.2 (TOU): demand charge on the highest 15-minute
  average in the peak window, peak and off-peak energy rates, service charge, power-factor charge for
  kVAR above 61.97% of kW, Ft per kWh and 7% VAT. A TOD view sits beside it from the same data.
- **One picker for everything.** Real-time row (kWh bars, consumption, 3-phase current) and money row
  (TOD table, TOU table with *Cost Time of Use*, peak-demand chart) all follow one date-range selector.
- **Handover at the panel.** I walked the hospital's engineers through each board and matched the
  meter on the door to the same number on the dashboard, so the team trusts what it sees.

## Delivery

- CTs and PAC5200 meters commissioned on the monitored boards, with phase and polarity checks
- Energy Manager PRO configured with the meters, TOU and TOD cost formulas and the hospital dashboard
- Walk-through and handover with the hospital's engineers in mid-December 2024

## Result

- Electricity use and cost visible per building and per period instead of one monthly figure
- Peak demand shown on a chart, so the costliest 15 minutes of the month can be found and moved
- A measured baseline for the hospital's energy-saving work and its new rooftop solar plant

The full story in Thai, with the system drawing, the formula table and CT wiring lessons, is in the
