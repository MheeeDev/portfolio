## Problem

A factory building commercial washers and dryers for export has to power-test every machine at the
voltage its market uses — from 110 V single phase to 460 V three phase, with full-load currents from a
few amps (gas-heated) to well over 100 A (electric-heated), and two supplies at once for stacked units.
Two new test stations for large dryers were needed, and leaving the circuit choice to operators meant
every test was a chance to energise a machine on the wrong supply or behind an oversized breaker.

## My role & scope

Controls engineer on the integrator's team for six jobs in the plant during 2026:

- **New test stations (main job)** — control design, design documents, PLC and HMI programming,
  SCADA handshakes, FAT
- **Power upgrades** — two test-stand power-supply upgrades: overload-detection program changes,
  HMI updates and commissioning
- **Gas valves** — commissioning on live test stands
- **Pick-to-light** — operator screens and logic for 37 slots on the plant's existing Ignition licence
- **Previous-station interlock** — Ignition to MySQL read, PLC step and Optix alarms

## Approach

- **Supply from transformers, choice from data.** 400 V from the MDB feeds five auto-transformers
  (200, 230, 380, 440, 460 V) and an isolation transformer (110 V). Each voltage splits into circuits
  by current band, each with its own MCB, relay, contactor and plug.
- **The model picks the circuit.** SCADA returns the scanned model's voltage, phase, full-load amps,
  single or stacked, and heater type; the PLC finds the matching row and the HMI tells the operator
  which plug to connect. No match stops the sequence with a model-data alarm.
- **One AOI, one step machine.** Login, station, model and serial scans, drawing confirmation,
  energise, run and result, with request/response handshakes and 10-second timeouts. Hardware trips
  latch and return to step 0; communication alarms warn without dropping the cycle.
- **Documents you can click.** Overview, editable configuration with one-click station replication,
  the sequence of operations with its Structured Text, and interactive HMI mockups — reviewed with
  the client before coding.

## Delivery

- **Test stations** — design website, PLC program and AOI, Optix screens and a 29-page FAT test sheet
- **Stacked-unit test stands (3)** — larger MCBs and contactors, new cable and plugs, overload program
  updated; installed from a Sunday with commissioning on day three
- **Product audit station** — converted to test both washers and stacked units, with a new MCC
  section, transformer and 10-inch HMI
- **Gas valves** — multicore cabling done in the lunch break on running stations, then commissioning
- **Pick-to-light** — 37 slots, barcode scanner and PC to the plant's track-and-trace standard
- **Previous-station interlock** — read-only MySQL connection, transaction groups, a new PLC check
  step and four specific alarms on the HMI

## Result

- Every export version can be tested on the same stations without the operator choosing a breaker
- A second station built by replicating the first station's configuration
- Upgrades and new functions delivered into a running plant with planned, short stops
- Parts that failed the previous station can no longer move on, and every check is recorded
